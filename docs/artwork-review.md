## Weakest three

1. **46 Blue-throated Macaw** — the closest sorting collision is 42 Blue-and-Gold Macaw. Both have a large blue head/back and yellow breast on the identical long-tailed silhouette. The cyan throat versus dark bib, and the absence versus presence of a green crown, provide real differences, but they occupy a small area at native size. This bird is satisfactory, with less immediate separation than the strongest roster members. A more visible throat boundary or distinctive head posture would improve it while preserving slot 46.
2. **44 Military Macaw** — the olive body differs from the brighter generic Macaw and teal-headed Hahn's, but the red forehead is tiny. At native size, the shared pale mask, hooked bill and macaw pose carry more visual weight than that forehead. A slightly broader, clearer red brow would strengthen its identity without changing its numeric roster position.
3. **47 Hahn's Macaw** — its teal crown and red shoulder are useful differences from generic Macaw and Military Macaw. The smaller bill/head helps at 96px, but its body still occupies nearly the same field as the full-sized macaws, making its naturally compact identity less forceful at native size. A visibly more compact overall build or shorter tail would make its identity more immediate.

These are relative weaknesses in an otherwise strong set. No bird looks unfinished or broken. The score does not imply that all 52 would be identified by species name by an unfamiliar player.

Reviewed `/tmp/bird-sort-art/gallery.png` and `Bird.tsx`, `ParrotArt.tsx`, `SongbirdArt.tsx`, `WildBirdArt.tsx`, `BirdDetails.tsx`, and `roster.ts`. I also inspected the gallery renderer to confirm that its native-size inset uses the actual family components at 48 × 56 with the same 96-square viewBox as `Bird`.

The set earns A on its visible merits. The shared ink, warm eye whites, restrained highlights, and feather treatment make the 52 birds feel like one illustrated collection. Strong silhouette changes carry owl, peacock, hummingbird, flamingo, pelican, toucan, duck, and swan even in the small insets. The less spectacular birds also have clear cues: the cardinal's mask and crest, goldfinch's black cap and wing, sparrow's facial pattern, and falcon's dark facial stripe survive the reduction. Kakapo and kea now read as different body types, rather than green macaw recolors. Generic names such as Macaw, Parrot, Blue Macaw, Parakeet, and Purple Parrot are acceptable under the brief; I did not penalize them for lacking a narrowly specified species identity.

The remaining deduction is mostly the cost of a large related-parrot roster at this scale. Blue-and-Gold Macaw (42) and Blue-throated Macaw (46) are the closest pair: the green crown/dark bib versus cyan crown/blue throat is visible, but asks more attention than most pairs. Macaw (3), Military Macaw (44), and Hahn's Macaw (47) also require learning their palette and head/shoulder cues. Amazon (36), Parakeet (37), and Eclectus (49) stay distinguishable through yellow cap, ring and long tail, and orange beak/tall build respectively, though some fine markings become subpixel texture. These are residual challenges, not identity failures. Repeated family geometry is appropriate here, and prominent color blocks still separate the birds.

No clipped crest, bill, tail, foot, or peacock train is visible in either gallery size. Source coordinates and the padded viewBox support the static rendering. This technical score covers the reviewed still artwork only; it does not claim verification of animated clipping in a running game.

**No artwork change is required to reach A.** Optional future polish would strengthen the crown/throat contrast of 42 versus 46 and simplify a few fine green-parrot markings into broader patches. Keep all existing roster slots and their numeric order.
